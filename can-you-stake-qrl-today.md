# Can You Stake QRL Today?

**Short answer.** Not on the QRL chain as it runs today, because consensus is proof of work and staking has no role in it. Proof of stake is part of the Zond design, which runs on a test network and has no mainnet date. Testnet participation is not yield, and no staking product on the current chain exists.

## Why there is no staking now

Staking means locking coins to earn the right to propose blocks, and it requires a chain whose consensus is proof of stake. QRL's consensus is proof of work, where blocks are produced by mining and security comes from the work behind the chain.

There is no place in that design for a staker. No contract holds your coins, no validator earns a share of emissions for holding them, and no protocol rule rewards a balance for sitting still.

That makes any offer to stake QRL today a claim about something that does not exist on this chain. It may be a product on another network, a token with a similar name, or a scheme that pays earlier participants with later deposits; none of those is staking QRL.

[Can You Mine QRL?](https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/can-you-mine-qrl.md) covers what does secure the chain, including the algorithm and the hardware it runs on.

## What Zond changes

Project Zond is the successor workstream, and its design is proof of stake. That is the part people hear about, and it is accurate as a description of the design rather than of anything running on mainnet.

Zond is also EVM-compatible and uses ML-DSA signatures, which are lattice-based rather than hash-based. Those choices are documented as the design of a separate network, not as changes to the chain you can use today.

The distinction to hold is between a design and a deployment. A proof-of-stake design implies validators, and validators imply staking; none of that exists until the network it belongs to is live. [What Is Project Zond for QRL?](https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/what-is-project-zond-for-qrl.md) covers what the workstream is.

## Testnet is not yield

A test network exists so that software can be tested. Its tokens are for testing, they carry no monetary value, and rewards earned there are not an asset you can sell.

It is worth being blunt about this, because testnets attract two kinds of confusion. The first is technical: a testnet balance is not a mainnet balance, and a wallet pointed at one will not show the other. The second is commercial: a page that offers "staking rewards" on a test network and asks for a deposit is not describing a test.

If you want to follow the work, following it is free. Anything that requires you to send value to participate in a test is not a test.

## What "staking" claims to watch for

Three patterns recur when a staking offer appears for an asset that has none.

The first is a yield figure with no source. An annual percentage is easy to print and hard to verify, and on a chain with no staking consensus there is nothing behind it.

The second is a deposit address rather than a contract or a validator. Legitimate staking on a proof-of-stake chain works through well-defined mechanisms; a personal address collecting deposits is a different thing entirely.

The third is urgency. A closing window, a limited allocation, a bonus for the first hundred participants: each is a way to compress the time you have to think.

[Avoid QRL Price Prediction Scams](https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/avoid-qrl-price-prediction-scams.md) covers how these pitches are built, and the rule for this one is simple: if a chain has no staking, an offer to stake its coin is not about the chain.

## Where to follow it

Progress on Zond appears in the project's repositories and documentation, and the release history of the wallet shows what has actually shipped. Those are checkable sources; a social post is not.

[Is the QRL Project Still Active?](https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/is-the-qrl-project-still-active.md) covers how to check activity in a few minutes, including the parts of the record that are easy to verify and the parts that require trusting a summary.

What none of those sources provides is a date. The project has not published a mainnet date for Zond, and a page that supplies one is supplying something it does not have.

## What this means for a holder

If you hold QRL expecting a yield, there is none on this chain. The balance earns nothing by sitting in a wallet, and the only way it grows is if you mine or if someone sends you coins.

That is not a defect to work around; it is what proof of work means. A chain that pays you for holding would be paying you from somewhere, and on a proof-of-work chain with a fixed cap the only source of new coins is block issuance.

For a holder, the useful questions are about custody rather than yield: is the phrase backed up, is the address's signature count healthy, and is the wallet on the current release. ## FAQ

### Can I stake QRL right now?

No. The chain's consensus is proof of work, and staking has no role in it.

### Will Zond support staking?

Proof of stake is part of the Zond design, so staking is implied by that design. It is not available until that network is live, and no mainnet date has been published.

### Can I earn rewards on the Zond testnet?

Test networks exist for testing, and their tokens carry no monetary value. Rewards there are not an asset you can sell.

### Someone is offering QRL staking with a fixed return. Is it real?

No. If the chain has no staking, an offer to stake its coin is not describing the chain. Treat it as a scheme and do not send value to it.
