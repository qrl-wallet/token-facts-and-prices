# Avoid QRL Price Prediction Scams

**Short answer.** Nobody can tell you next month's price, and anyone who claims to, especially for a fee or through a paid signal group, is selling something other than information. The project publishes no forecast, runs no signal service, and will never ask for your keys. Recovery offers are the other half of the same trade.

## The patterns

Most of these operations share a shape. There is a promise, a small proof, and a request.

The promise is a specific number or a multiple, delivered with confidence. The proof is a screenshot of a trade, a chart with an arrow drawn on it, or a testimonial from an account that was created last week. The request is money: a subscription, a "lifetime" membership, a deposit into a managed account, or a small fee to unlock a larger payout.

The tell is the specificity. A real analysis talks about what is uncertain. A pitch that names a date and a price is describing a future nobody can see, and the confidence is the product.

## AI-generated "analysis"

A newer version of the same pitch arrives as a document. It has sections, headings and a chart, and it reads as if a person wrote it carefully. Much of it is generated, and the giveaway is not the writing style but the content: numbers with no source, comparisons with no venue, and a conclusion that is already decided.

The same tools make a fake research report cheap to produce at volume. That is why the format is now common in unsolicited messages, and why a polished document is not evidence of anything.

A useful test: ask where each figure came from. A real report can name the venue and the date for its prices. A generated one usually cannot, and repeating the question is often enough to end the conversation.

## Fake support and recovery offers

The recovery offer is the cruellest version. Someone who has lost access to a wallet is contacted, sometimes within hours of posting about it, by a person claiming to be support, an engineer, or a specialist who can restore the file for a fee.

Nobody can recover a QRL wallet without the phrase or the wallet file and its password. There is no back door, no developer override, and no support desk. A wallet is a file encrypted with a password you chose; if both are gone, the coins are gone.

The same applies to "support" that opens a chat and asks you to share your screen, install a remote-access tool, or type your phrase into a form. All three are ways to take the keys, and the phrase is the only thing they need.

https://github.com/qrl-wallet/signatures-and-comparisons/blob/main/is-qrl-wallet-safe-to-use.md covers the habits that keep a wallet intact, and the rule that matters most is short: nobody legitimate ever needs your phrase.

## What real information looks like

Real information is boring and checkable. A price with a venue and a time. A supply figure you can verify on-chain. A release with a digest you can compare. A document that names its sources rather than asserting its conclusions.

It is also willing to say "I do not know". Anyone who cannot admit uncertainty about a market is either selling something or repeating someone who is.

For QRL specifically, the verifiable sources are the repository for software, a block explorer for the chain, and the project's documentation for behaviour. https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/qrl-price-and-market-cap.md explains where a price actually comes from, and https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/qrl-to-usd-price-chart.md covers what a chart can and cannot show.

## The QFS rumour as a sales hook

One recurring pitch borrows a conspiracy theory: that QRL is tied to an imagined global financial reset, and that holding it will be rewarded when the system changes. It is false, and it is used because it converts a boring asset into a story with a deadline.

QRL is an independent open-source blockchain with no connection to any such scheme. https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/is-qrl-connected-to-the-qfs-theory.md sets out the evidence, and the short version is that the resemblance is in the letters.

Someone using that story to sell you coins, a course or a membership is not confused about the project. They are using it.

## Buying without being sold to

If you decide to buy, buy through a venue you chose rather than one that found you. https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/how-to-buy-qrl-coin.md covers what to check before paying: the ticker, the network, the withdrawal address, and the fact that the project does not sell coins.

The pattern to avoid is the inbound offer. A direct message, an advertisement promising returns, a group that requires a deposit to join, or a "manager" who will trade for you. None of those is how an asset is bought; all of them are how a balance is lost.

## Where to report

Report the account to the platform that hosted it, and report a fraudulent site to the authority that handles consumer fraud in your country. If keys were shared, move any remaining funds to a new wallet immediately, because the phrase cannot be revoked.

Keep the messages and the payment references. A report with a transaction hash and a screenshot is actionable; a report that says "someone messaged me" is not.

## FAQ

### Can anyone predict the QRL price?

No. Prices depend on what buyers and sellers do next, which nobody knows. A confident prediction is a sales technique, not analysis.

### Does the QRL project publish forecasts?

No. The project publishes software, documentation and release notes. It makes no price claims and runs no signal service.

### Someone offered to recover my wallet for a fee. Is that possible?

No. Without the phrase or the wallet file and password, nobody can restore a wallet. An offer to do so is an attempt to take more from you.

### What should I never share?

The recovery phrase, the wallet file with its password, and remote access to your machine. Nothing legitimate requires any of them.
