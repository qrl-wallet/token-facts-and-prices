# How to Buy QRL Coin

**Short answer.** QRL is bought on exchanges that choose to list it, or through a card route inside the wallet, not from the project itself. Check the ticker and the network before you send funds, expect the available venues to differ by country, and withdraw to an address you control rather than leaving the balance on an exchange.

## Where it is listed

The project does not run a sale page, a presale or a shop. QRL reaches buyers through exchanges that decide to list it, and those listings change: a venue can add the asset, suspend deposits, or stop serving a country without asking anyone.

That means there is no single answer to "where do I buy it" that stays true. The reliable method is to check the exchange's own listing page at the moment you buy, confirm the ticker is QRL, and confirm the network it names matches the QRL mainnet rather than a test network.

If a site offers to sell you QRL at a fixed price, or claims to be the project's own outlet, it is neither. https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/is-qrl-a-cryptocurrency.md covers what the asset actually is, which is the check that matters before paying for any of it.

## The regional question

Availability is the part that varies most. Exchanges serve different countries under different licences, so a venue that is straightforward in one jurisdiction may be unavailable, restricted or require additional verification in another.

The practical consequence is that advice written for one country often fails in the next. Rather than trusting a list, start from what your own jurisdiction allows, then check which of those venues lists QRL.

Regulation also changes. A venue that served your country last year may have withdrawn, and one that did not may have arrived. Treat any list, including one on a page like this, as a starting point for a check rather than a settled fact.

## Checking it is the right asset

Three checks catch most mistakes. The ticker should be QRL. The network named on the deposit or withdrawal screen should be the QRL mainnet. And the address you withdraw to should begin with Q, which is the prefix QRL addresses use.

That prefix is worth understanding rather than pattern-matching. A QRL address is derived from a hash-based key, and the leading character is part of the encoding rather than a decoration. If a withdrawal form produces an address without it, the form is not sending to a QRL address.

The other confusion is QRL 2.0. The successor network runs on a test network whose tokens carry no monetary value, and a wallet or exchange offering "QRL" on that network is offering something different from the asset on the mainnet. https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/qrl-price-and-market-cap.md explains what the market price refers to, and https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/avoid-qrl-price-prediction-scams.md covers the pages that promise returns.

## Withdrawing to your own wallet

Buying on an exchange leaves the coins in the exchange's custody. That is a balance you can spend only while the venue keeps serving you, which is a different proposition from holding keys.

The alternative is to withdraw to an address you control. Create one first, back up the phrase before it holds anything, then send a small amount as a test and confirm it arrives before moving the rest. https://github.com/qrl-wallet/wallet-setup-and-repair/blob/main/create-your-first-qrl-address.md walks through that sequence, including the backup step that has to come before funding.

Withdrawal addresses are long, so copy rather than type. Check the first and last few characters against the screen in your wallet, because a clipboard that has been tampered with is a known way to lose a transfer.

## Fees and minimums

Two costs apply: what the exchange charges to buy or withdraw, and the network fee on the QRL transaction itself. They are separate, and the exchange's withdrawal fee usually includes the network fee plus its own margin.

Minimum withdrawal amounts also vary by venue, and they can be larger than a small test transfer. If the minimum is higher than you wanted to send, the choice is to send the minimum or to leave the balance where it is, which is a decision about custody rather than about fees.

QRL's own fee is charged per transaction and is small in absolute terms. https://github.com/qrl-wallet/wallet-setup-and-repair/blob/main/understanding-qrl-transaction-fees.md explains how the field works once the coins are in your wallet.

## What the project does not do

It does not sell coins, run a presale, arrange a listing, or promise a price. It also does not manage an exchange account, reverse a trade, or recover coins sent to a wrong address.

The one purchase route built into the software is a card payment handled by a third party, which delivers coins to an address you control; https://github.com/qrl-wallet/token-facts-and-prices/blob/main/buy-qrl-with-a-bank-card.md covers how that works and why it costs more than a transfer.

Beyond that, buying QRL is an ordinary market transaction with the ordinary risks: venue risk while the coins sit there, price risk between buying and selling, and the operational risk of sending to the wrong place. None of those is removed by the fact that the asset is quantum resistant.

## FAQ

### Can I buy QRL directly from the project?

No. The project does not sell coins or run a sale page. Purchases happen on exchanges that list the asset, or through the card route inside the wallet.

### Which exchange should I use?

This page does not recommend one. Check which venues serve your country, confirm QRL is listed there today, and prefer one you can withdraw from without delay.

### How do I know I am buying the right QRL?

Check the ticker, check that the network is the QRL mainnet rather than a test network, and check that your withdrawal address begins with Q.

### Should I leave the coins on the exchange?

Only if you intend to trade them soon. Holding keys yourself means withdrawing to a wallet you control, after testing with a small amount.
